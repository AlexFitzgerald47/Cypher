# Workstream D — The McGuire postcard (1920)

**Status: SUBSTANTIALLY READ (2019, credit below) — this session adjudicated the state,
confirmed the substitution is consistent, identified the system's character, and defined
the one physical step that closes it. Companion result: the 1927 IRA message from
Schmeh's 2018 post is SOLVED (Bean/Gillogly 2019) and is off the target list.**

## The artifact

Easter-motif postcard ("Wishing You Easter Joy / Happy may your Easter be…"), sent from
the UK to Mr. P. J. Maguire, Killycloghan, Tully—, Co. Fermanagh; postmark November 1920;
halfpenny green (George V), stamp affixed upside-down. Recipient: Patrick James McGuire,
Scottish Brigade IRA, 1916 Easter Rising veteran. Surfaced by historian Stephen Coyle on
behalf of McGuire's daughter; posed by Klaus Schmeh, Cipherbrain, 2019-10-02.
Message side: salutation + ~10 short lines in an invented symbol alphabet, words
separated by periods. Images: `card_text_side.png` (best available), `IMG_1024-26.jpeg`,
crops in this folder.

## State of the solve (prior art — the 2019 comment thread)

Richard SantaColoma, with corrections from x3Ray, read the message by visual pattern
matching (never published a key table):

> Dear Paddy, / Hope you are set as I am. / So my self hopeing you will enjoy yoursele
> this coming Easser [Easter]. / Yours, David.

("yoursele"/"Easser" = sender's spellings; lines 7–8 contain author's cross-outs.)

## What this session verified and added

1. **The substitution is internally consistent** where resolution permits: the O-glyph
   (a stroke with crossbar, ᚮ-like) recurs identically in HOPE and YOU; word divisions
   (periods) match the reading's word lengths line by line.
2. **System identification, negative and positive**: it is NOT ogham (glyphs carry
   crossbars, flags and T/Γ forms; ogham is stemline tallies) and NOT standard Younger
   Futhark, but a private runic-style straight-stroke alphabet — the kind two comrades
   would share from a copied key sheet. Ticks/flags distinguish letters within a family
   (E/F/U forms differ by tick count), which is exactly where the sender's "misspellings"
   (YOURSELE for yourself, EASSER for Easter) sit — likely one-tick slips, not orthography.
3. **The 2019 reading stands.** Nothing in the better crops contradicts it; the residuals
   (line-initial glyphs, the crossed-out characters in lines 7–8, a complete key table)
   are limited by scan resolution, not by cryptanalysis.
4. **Historical frame** (kept minimal): an Easter card postmarked November 1920 — the
   bloodiest month of the War of Independence — to a Rising veteran, "hope you are set
   as I am", with the King's stamp inverted. The Easter-in-November anomaly plus period
   slang "set" (ready/prepared) permit but do not prove an operational reading; the
   surface reading as a comradely greeting is complete and coherent. Do not overclaim.

## What closes it

A 600-dpi scan of the message side from the original card (via Stephen Coyle / the
McGuire family). With that: full glyph segmentation, a complete symbol→letter key
(publishable as the first key table for this cipher), the crossed-out lines recovered,
and the sender "David" possibly identifiable via Scottish Brigade rolls (Coyle's
speciality). Draft note to Coyle is in ../REPORT.md.

## Companion adjudication: the 1927 IRA message (Schmeh 2018-09-03)

The one message of ~1,300 that Gillogly could not break (151 letters, vowel-rich
transposition) was SOLVED in the post's own comment thread (Aug 2019) by Richard Bean:
width-12 columnar with the 11th column omitted in copying, endorsed by Gillogly —
"RE GELIGNIT[E] SCOTLAND STA[TE] THEY RAIDE[D] AND OBTAINED [S]OME OF TH[I]S…".
Not an open target. Source PDF: `../sources/schmeh_2018_ira_message_post.pdf`.
